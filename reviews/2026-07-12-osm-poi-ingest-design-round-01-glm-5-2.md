HIGH | Section 5 | Overpass relation geometry handling is underspecified: relations can contain members of mixed types (ways and nodes) and incomplete member geometries, yet the design only says "request geometry with `out geom`" and skip if incomplete. Overpass `out geom` does not always resolve nested relation members or role-filtered members for multipolygons. REQUIRED EDIT: Specify that relation members must be recursively resolved (e.g., `out geom;` preceded by adequate `relation`/`way`/`node` recursion or `out geom tags;` with explicit member fetching), and define the exact completeness check (all member roles present, all member geometries non-empty) before attempting Shapely polygonization.

MEDIUM | Section 4 | `nearest_edge: tuple[int, int]` implies edge keys are integer node UIDs, but pound graph edges may carry multi-attribute keys or parallel edges; storing a tuple of node UIDs can collide or lose edge identity when multiple edges share the same endpoints. REQUIRED EDIT: Confirm pound graph edges are uniquely identified by a `(u, v)` UID pair with no parallel-edge ambiguity, or change `nearest_edge` to include the NetworkX edge key (e.g., `tuple[int, int, int | None]`) and update validation accordingly.

MEDIUM | Section 6 | `nearest_coord_candidates()` uses a conservative lon/lat envelope but does not state the STRtree is built with node **points** in `(lon, lat)` order while haversine distances are computed from `(lat, lon)` — the two coordinate orders are a known silent-swap risk and the asymmetric test is mentioned only for edges, not for node-index construction. REQUIRED EDIT: Add an explicit asymmetric node-index test requirement and state that `nearest_coord_candidates()` converts each STRtree-hit Shapely `(lon, lat)` point back to `(lat, lon)` before calling `_haversine_m()`.
